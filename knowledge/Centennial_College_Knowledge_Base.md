## SECTION 1: INSTITUTIONAL OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Founding and Historical Context

Centennial College is Ontario's first community college. It was established in 1966, created as part of the province's new system of Colleges of Applied Arts and Technology (CAATs). That system was established on May 21, 1965, under the direction of the Hon. William Davis, who was Ontario's Minister of Education at the time (and later Premier of Ontario). Centennial's first site, the Warden Woods Campus, opened on October 17, 1966, in a converted, decommissioned federal government building. The original enrolment was 514 students across 16 career-oriented programs, including journalism, secretarial science, and early childhood education.

From these roots, the college's programming expanded steadily into health and nutrition, hospitality, child studies, and community services. In 1973, responsibility for nursing education in Ontario was transferred from hospitals to colleges, and most of Centennial's programs relocated into the newly built Centennial Science and Technology Centre — the building that is known today as the Morningside Campus.

Centennial has continued to expand internationally. It has held a presence in international markets including China, India, South Korea, Brazil, and the United Arab Emirates through partnerships, recruitment offices, and articulated programs. Its most prominent overseas partnership is **Suzhou Centennial College**, located in the Dushu Lake Higher Education Town in Suzhou, China — a hub with more than 100,000 students — where Ontario College credentials are delivered through partner programming from Centennial's business school, engineering technology school, and hospitality/tourism/culinary school.

### 1.2 Mission, Vision, and Identity

- **Vision:** "Transforming lives and communities through learning."
- **Mission:** To educate students for career success, making decisions that support both the career and personal development of learners while valuing the diverse profiles of its student body.
- **Motto (formal, historic):** *Transforming Lives and Communities Through Learning* — associated with Centennial College of Applied Arts and Technology, the college's full legal name.
- **Nickname / athletics identity:** The Centennial **Colts**; mascot is **Bolt the Colt**; official colour is green.
- **Cultural diversity:** Centennial is consistently described as one of the most culturally diverse post-secondary institutions in Canada, with representation from almost 100 ethno-cultural groups and roughly 80 languages spoken on campus.

### 1.3 Scale and Structure

As of the college's current public materials, Centennial operates through **five campuses and seven satellite/specialized locations**, serving the eastern Greater Toronto Area (chiefly Scarborough, with an additional aerospace-focused site in North York/Downsview). Historic materials sometimes describe "four campuses," reflecting the fact that the campus count has grown over time as the college has consolidated or added specialized sites (e.g., the Downsview aerospace campus and the Performing Arts Centre). CampusAI should treat the **current, authoritative count as five campuses** (Progress, Morningside, Ashtonbee, Story Arts Centre, and Downsview), plus additional specialized/satellite sites such as the Performing Arts Centre and the Toronto Airways Inc. flight-training site.

Enrolment figures vary by source and by how "student" is counted (headcount vs. full-time equivalent, credit vs. non-credit). Commonly cited figures include roughly 19,000–26,000 combined full-time and part-time students in a given year, more than 12,000 international and exchange students on campus in recent years, over 260 programs (credentials spanning certificates, diplomas, advanced diplomas, honours bachelor's degrees, and graduate certificates), and a cumulative alumni base exceeding 90,000–100,000 graduates. Because these figures shift from year to year and depend on methodology, CampusAI should present them as approximate and avoid stating a single precise enrolment number as if it were fixed.

### 1.4 Governance and Leadership

- Centennial is governed by a **Board of Governors** made up of 12 volunteers appointed from the external community (plus the President and elected employee/student representatives), which is the college's paramount governing body under the authority of the Ontario Minister responsible for colleges.
- The **President and CEO** is **Dr. Craig Stephenson**, who assumed the presidency on August 26, 2019, following an extensive national search. He had previously joined Centennial in 2008 as Dean of Students and later served as Vice-President, Student and Community Engagement. His presidency has been particularly associated with the college's COVID-19 response (including development of dozens of online programs and micro-credentials, and hosting a large-scale vaccination site in partnership with Scarborough Health Network at the Progress Campus).
- Below the President, Centennial's **Executive Team** consists of three Vice-Presidents, two Associate Vice-Presidents, and a Chief of Staff, meeting weekly on institutional matters.

### 1.5 Rankings and Research Reputation

Centennial does not appear in traditional university-style global rankings (such as QS or Times Higher Education) in a way that is meaningful for a career-college, since those systems are built around research universities. Where Centennial's reputation is best documented is in **applied research performance**, tracked annually by **Research Infosource Inc.**, which publishes "Canada's Top 50 Research Colleges." Centennial has repeatedly ranked at or near the top nationally in specific categories:

- **#1 in Canada for paid student research internships** for multiple consecutive years — for example, 599 paid student researchers were engaged through the college's Applied Research, Innovation and Entrepreneurship (ARIES) unit ("Centennial Innovates") in a recent fiscal year, a figure the college has cited as a fourth consecutive year holding the national leadership position in this category, following 571 in an earlier year and 515 the year before that.
- Strong standing in **industry research income as a share of total research income** (Centennial has cited figures near or above 45–49% in some years, among the highest of any large-tier college).
- Placement within the **Top 50 Research Colleges** overall (specific numeric rank in "overall research revenue" has varied year to year — commonly cited in roughly the high teens to low twenties, e.g., 20th or 23rd overall depending on the fiscal year cited).
- Additional accolades cited by the college include recognition as a Canada's Top Employer / "Top 100" competitor employer in some years, a CICan Gold Medal for Internationalization Excellence, and consistently high employer-satisfaction survey results (the college has cited employer satisfaction figures in the high-80s to mid-90s percentage range in different years' Ontario KPI-style surveys — treat any single percentage figure as a snapshot rather than a permanent statistic).

CampusAI should describe Centennial's research standing using the *category-specific* language above (e.g., "ranked #1 in Canada for paid student research internships") rather than implying it holds a single overall "top college" rank, since the underlying rankings are broken out by category and by institutional size tier (Large / Medium / Small).

## SECTION 3: ACADEMIC STRUCTURE — FACULTIES AND SCHOOLS

### 3.1 Important Structural Note for CampusAI

Centennial's academic organization has been restructured. Historically (and in many third-party directories, program-comparison sites, and older cached college pages that are still circulating online), Centennial's academic units were commonly described as **seven (or sometimes eight) specialized academic schools** reporting more or less directly to the college, with names such as "School of Engineering Technology and Applied Science," "School of Business," "School of Communications, Media and Design," "School of Community and Health Studies," "School of Transportation," and "School of Hospitality, Tourism and Culinary Arts."

As of the college's **current official site structure**, Centennial organizes its academic programs under **four faculties**, each of which contains multiple named schools — roughly 18 schools in total across the four faculties. This is the structure CampusAI should treat as authoritative when a user asks "what school is my program in," while still recognizing the older names since they may appear in a user's offer letter, an older brochure, or a third-party source the user is looking at. A rough mapping of old-to-new naming is provided in 3.2–3.5 below, but the *safest* approach for CampusAI is to describe the **current faculty/school name** and, where helpful, mention the historic name in parentheses.

The four current faculties are:

1. **Faculty of Global Business and Creative Industries (GBCI)**
2. **Faculty of Integrated Health and Community Care**
3. **Faculty of Lifelong Learning and Industry Training (LLIT)**
4. **Faculty of Applied Sciences, Engineering Technology, Trades and Transportation**

- **Industry Training** — customized, employer-facing training programs.

#### Program Overview (In Centennial's Own Framing, Paraphrased)

The program is positioned as training for the applied engineering of AI systems — not just the use of AI tools. The curriculum is organized by the college into four broad pillars:

1. **Foundational AI and Machine Learning** — supervised, unsupervised, self-supervised, transfer, and reinforcement learning theory, implemented hands-on with frameworks such as Scikit-learn, TensorFlow, and PyTorch; plus MLOps concepts (production ML pipelines) using tools such as Airflow, TensorFlow TFX, and SageMaker Pipelines.
2. **Data Engineering and Scalable Systems** — big-data analytics (Spark, Hadoop ecosystem), data-pipeline design, relational databases (Oracle, MS-SQL Server) and NoSQL databases (MongoDB), vector databases (e.g., Chroma DB) for embeddings/RAG/semantic search, cloud-native AI architecture, API-centric AI integration (vision/speech/language APIs), and managed cloud ML lifecycle tools (e.g., SageMaker).
3. **Cutting-Edge AI Specializations** — generative AI and large language models, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), agentic AI workflows (LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI), intelligent robotics (behaviour trees and LLMs within the ROS 2 framework), advanced natural-language-processing chatbot development, and recommender-systems design.
4. **Professional Software Engineering** — requirements engineering, Agile project delivery, full-stack AI application integration, programming proficiency across Python, PySpark, JavaScript, C#, Java, and Kotlin, AI-assisted coding tools (e.g., GitHub Copilot), AI ethics/bias/data-governance practice, and human-centred design for AI-powered interfaces.

The program culminates in a **Capstone Project** in which students build an original AI solution addressing a genuine business problem, intended as a portfolio piece for job-seeking.

#### Admission Requirements

- Ontario Secondary School Diploma (OSSD) or equivalent, **or** mature-student status (19 years of age or older).
- Grade 12 English (C or U) or equivalent minimum grade — **or** passing the Centennial English Admission Test through the Assessment Centre.
- Grade 11 Mathematics (M or U) or Grade 12 Mathematics (C or U), or equivalent — **or** passing Centennial's Engineering Math Skills Assessment for Admission.
- Standard English-language-proficiency documentation for applicants whose prior education was not in English (see Section 4).

#### Co-op Option

Co-op is **optional** for this program. Students who wish to pursue it typically apply during their **second semester**; academically qualified applicants are admitted into the co-op preparation course. Requirements to be eligible to apply include:

- Completion of at least 80% of the combined Semester 1 and Semester 2 course load.
- A grade of C (60%) or higher in COMM-160/161 by the end of Semester 2.
- A cumulative GPA of 2.5 or higher, which must be **maintained throughout the program**.
- Legal eligibility to work in Canada.

Meeting these minimums does **not guarantee** admission into the co-op stream, since co-op seats are competitive. Students apply to the co-op stream through the Career Services & Cooperative Education Department.

**Co-op sequencing (Fall-semester intake route):** Semester 1 (Fall) → Semester 2 (Winter) → Break (Summer) → Semester 3 (Fall) → Co-op Work Term 1 (Winter) → Semester 4 (Summer) → Co-op Work Term 2 (Fall) → Semester 5 (Winter) → Co-op Work Term 3 (Summer) → Semester 6 (Fall).

**Co-op sequencing (Winter-semester intake route):** Semester 1 (Winter) → Semester 2 (Summer) → Semester 3 (Fall) → Co-op Work Term 1 (Winter) → Semester 4 (Summer) → Co-op Work Term 2 (Fall) → Semester 5 (Winter) → Co-op Work Term 3 (Summer) → Semester 6 (Fall).

This means eligible co-op students in this program complete **three separate work terms**, which aligns with the general Centennial pattern of co-op students completing work terms in 4-month blocks (see Section 7 for tax-credit and hiring rules tied to these work-term lengths).

## SECTION 4: ADMISSIONS AND REGISTRATION

### 4.1 General Admission Framework (All Applicant Types)

Admission requirements vary by program, so CampusAI should always frame general answers as a baseline and encourage users to confirm the specific program's page. That said, the college describes the following general framework:

- **Certificate, Diploma, or Advanced Diploma programs** generally require an Ontario Secondary School Diploma (OSSD) or an OSSD-equivalent credential.
- **Degree (Bachelor's) programs** generally require the OSSD (or equivalent) with academic requirements met at the "U" (University) or "M" (University/College) course level, typically including a minimum number of specific "12U/12M/OAC"-type credits.
- **Graduate Certificate programs** generally require a completed three-year college diploma or university degree from a recognized postsecondary institution (program-specific GPA minimums, e.g., around 2.0 on a 4.3 scale, or higher, may apply, along with minimum credit-hour thresholds).
- Additional program-specific requirements may include portfolios, auditions, writing samples, math tests, interviews, or attendance at an on-campus admission session — always check the specific program listing.

### 4.2 Domestic High-School Students

Current Ontario high-school students and recent Ontario high-school graduates apply through **Ontariocolleges.ca**, notify their school's guidance counsellor that they've applied, and the school forwards transcripts directly to Centennial via Ontariocolleges.ca. Domestic applicants from Canadian high schools **outside** Ontario, or from a Canadian college/university, are responsible for requesting that their institution send required transcripts/documents to Ontariocolleges.ca themselves.

For many programs, applicants who do not have the specific English or Math course-level prerequisite can instead take a **Centennial College skills assessment** (through the Assessment Centre) to demonstrate equivalent competency — although some programs (notably Practical Nursing and all applied-degree programs) require the actual course credit and do not accept the skills-assessment alternative.

**Application timing:** There is no single universal deadline, but Centennial recommends applying **before February 1** for a Fall-term start, since many programs fill months in advance and the college begins waitlisting or closing popular programs as early as March for a September start. Applications submitted after February 1 are still accepted and considered, but on a **first-come, first-served** basis against remaining seats.

**Application fee:** A non-refundable fee (commonly cited at **$150 CAD** via Ontariocolleges.ca for domestic applicants in some materials, though the college's own program pages cite a **$125 CAD** figure in the international/direct-application context — confirm the current fee for the applicant's specific channel) allows an applicant to apply to up to five programs through Ontariocolleges.ca.

### 4.3 Mature Students

Applicants who do **not** hold an OSSD or equivalent may apply as a **mature student** if they are **19 years of age or older** for most certificate/diploma/advanced-diploma programs (a small number of programs set the mature-applicant threshold at **21 years of age or older** instead — always confirm per-program). Mature applicants are still required to satisfy the same program-specific academic prerequisites (e.g., English and Math course-level equivalents), which they may demonstrate via the Centennial skills-assessment process described above, unless the specific program requires actual course credit. Applicants **under 19** must show proof of successful OSSD completion; the mature-applicant pathway is not available to them.

### 4.4 International Students and Credential Assessment (WES / ICAS)

International applicants — and any applicant whose prior education occurred outside Canada — must have their transcripts independently assessed by one of two credential-assessment agencies recognized by the college:

- **World Education Services (WES)**
- **International Credential Assessment Service of Canada (ICAS)**

For applicants going through **Ontariocolleges.ca**, the WES/ICAS assessment is submitted alongside the standard application. For applicants applying **directly to Centennial** as international students (via the college's own **ApplyCentennial** portal), the Admissions Office may separately request official transcripts or credential evaluations. Similarly, students requesting **transfer credit** for a prior international course must supply either an ICAS post-secondary comprehensive report or a WES course-by-course report to support the request (alongside grade-equivalency and contact-hour-matching rules — core courses must generally have been completed within 5 years of the application semester, and general-education/communication courses within 10 years).

**Other international-admissions essentials:**
- **Application fee:** CAD $125, non-refundable (the college notes this fee may be waived for students who are already in Canada holding a valid study permit, in some third-party summaries — confirm current policy).
- **English-language proficiency:** IELTS, TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, or Duolingo English Test scores are accepted; specific minimum scores vary by program and credential level (fast-track and degree-level programs generally require higher scores than standard diploma programs).
- **Designated Learning Institution (DLI) status:** Centennial is a DLI, a requirement for issuing documentation international students need for their Canadian study permit application.
- **Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL):** International applicants may need a PAL to study in Ontario; at the study-permit stage, PGWP eligibility is *not* itself a study-permit requirement — Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) will approve study permits for any full-time Centennial program (PGWP-eligible or not) provided general study-permit requirements are met.
- **Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) eligibility rules changed in late 2024:** Students who applied for their **study permit before November 1, 2024** remain PGWP-eligible across *all* of Centennial's full-time programs, regardless of field of study. Students applying **on or after November 1, 2024** have PGWP eligibility that depends on whether their specific program aligns with Canada's designated priority labour-market categories — Centennial publishes a list of "PGWP-Aligned Programs" (certificate, diploma, advanced diploma, degree, and graduate-certificate levels) and flags PGWP alignment directly on each program's page (e.g., the AI program in Section 3.6 above is currently flagged **"PGWP Aligned: Yes"**).

### 4.5 How to Apply — Step-by-Step (Both Domestic and International)

1. **Apply online.** Domestic applicants not going through Better Jobs Ontario or an internal program transfer apply via **Ontariocolleges.ca**. International applicants apply directly at **ApplyCentennial** (accessible via centennialcollege.ca/international-education/how-to-apply-to-centennial-college), paying the non-refundable $125 CAD fee online.
2. **Submit supporting documents.** Ontario high-school applicants rely on their guidance counsellor to forward transcripts; out-of-province/out-of-country applicants (domestic or international) submit their own transcripts and, where applicable, WES/ICAS assessments.
3. **Confirm the Offer of Admission.** Offers are sent by mail; applicants must log in to their Ontariocolleges.ca (or ApplyCentennial) account and confirm before the "Deadline to Confirm" printed on the offer letter. An applicant may hold only **one** active college/program confirmation at a time. Missing the confirmation deadline risks losing the seat to another applicant (though a late confirmation may sometimes still be honoured if space remains, or the applicant may be waitlisted).
4. **Pay fees.** Fee statements are emailed only (never mailed on paper) to the student's personal and myCentennial email addresses. A minimum payment must be made by the "Fees Deadline" on the statement, or the seat may be reassigned.
5. **Build your timetable / register for courses** through **myCentennial** (my.centennialcollege.ca). Web-registration will not open until minimum fees are paid (or alternate arrangements made) and any conditional-offer conditions are satisfied.

Students who previously attended a full-time Centennial program may be eligible for an internal **Program Transfer** rather than a fresh application — this is arranged through **Enrolment Services** at any campus.

### 4.6 Accommodations During the Admissions Process

Applicants with a documented disability (learning disability, medical or mental-health condition, or physical, visual, hearing, or mobility impairment) who need testing accommodations during the admissions process are supported by the **Centre for Accessible Learning and Counselling Services (CALCS)** — see Section 5.4 for direct contact details.

To be eligible to hire a co-op student, an employer must commit to providing genuinely full-time work for the entire committed work-term length.

### 7.4 Ontario Co-operative Education Tax Credit (Employer-Facing)

Ontario offers a **refundable tax credit** to eligible employers who hire a student from a recognized post-secondary co-operative education program, intended to help students gain work experience and improve future employability. Key mechanics, as summarized by Centennial for prospective employer partners:

- **Credit amount:** the *lesser* of (a) **25% of eligible expenditures** (30% for small businesses) tied to that work placement, or (b) **$3,000** per qualifying co-op work placement.
- **Qualifying work term:** a minimum of **420 hours and 12 consecutive weeks** of work, and the placement must be a graduation requirement of a recognized co-op program. Where a placement runs longer than four months, each subsequent four-month period counts as a **separate** qualifying work term for tax-credit purposes.
- **Eligible expenditures** include salaries and wages (including taxable benefits) reported on the student's T4 slip.
- **Eligible employer:** any Ontario corporation or unincorporated business with a permanent establishment in Ontario, subject to Ontario corporate or personal income tax.
- **Documentation:** Centennial issues a **Confirmation of Employment letter** upon placement, which is sufficient supporting documentation for the employer's tax claim. The employer does not need to attach this letter to the tax return itself, but must retain it in case of a CRA or Ontario Ministry of Finance review.
- **Program eligibility note:** all of Centennial's **three-year Business and Engineering Technology and Applied Sciences co-op programs** qualify for this credit.
- **Employer contact for details:** Career Services & Co-operative Education, 416-289-5209, cooped@centennialcollege.ca.

**Additional employer-facing funding** cited by the college includes the **Colleges and Institutes Canada (CICan) Career-Launcher Internships program**, a separate federal-adjacent subsidy that can provide up to **$32,000** toward an intern's salary and training expenses — a distinct program from the Ontario provincial tax credit above, with its own application/terms process.

### 7.5 International Students: Co-op, SIN, and PGWP Rules

**Working while studying (non-co-op):** International students holding a **valid study permit** with off-campus work authorization may generally work during the academic year and during scheduled breaks, subject to the conditions printed on their study permit. Employers can confirm a given international student's eligibility by reviewing the study permit itself.

**Co-op / field-placement work specifically requires an additional, separate work permit.** For a co-op placement to be authorized, all of the following must be true:
- The student holds a **valid study permit** *and* a **valid co-op work permit**.
- The intended employment is an **essential, certified part of the student's program of study** (confirmed by a letter from a responsible academic official at the college).
- The co-op/internship employment **cannot exceed 50% of the total program of study's length**.

Employers hiring an international co-op student should review the student's valid study permit (with off-campus work authorization) alongside their co-op work permit specifically. Questions can be directed to Career Services & Co-operative Education at 416-289-5209 / cooped@centennialcollege.ca, or to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) directly.

**Social Insurance Number (SIN):** A SIN is a required 9-digit number for anyone working in Canada, issued to one person only and non-transferable. To apply, an applicant needs (a) a valid primary identity/legal-status document, (b) a valid secondary identity document, and (c) proof of address. Permanent residents provide their IRCC-issued Confirmation of Permanent Residence; temporary residents (including international students) provide their IRCC-issued study permit, work permit, or visitor record showing work authorization in Canada. IRCC can be reached at 1-888-242-2100 for eligibility questions.

**Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP):** International graduates apply for a PGWP to work in Canada after completing studies. Centennial's international-student advising team runs drop-in advising sessions and information webinars specifically on the PGWP process, and recommends students begin PGWP-related job-search preparation *before* their final semester ends, since document preparation takes time. See Section 4.4 above for the PGWP-eligibility policy change affecting study permits issued on or after November 1, 2024, and the concept of "PGWP-Aligned Programs."

**Employers wishing to retain an international graduate beyond their PGWP** can explore sponsoring an employer-specific work permit or pursuing options such as a Provincial Nominee Program pathway — the college recommends consulting an immigration representative for these more complex, longer-term routes.

### 8.3 Innovation Areas / Research Centres

Centennial Innovates organizes its work across roughly eight thematic innovation areas, several of which map to named, addressable research centres:

- **Wearable, Interactive and Mobile Technology Access Centre in Healthcare (WIMTACH)** — Centennial's flagship digital-health research centre, originally established through a $1.75 million federal grant (around 2015). The college reports a **100% success rate of WIMTACH student researchers being hired** after graduation.
- **Centre for Explainable Data Analytics (CEDA)** — builds intelligent/AI-driven decision-support systems specifically for small- and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).
- **Social Innovation Research Centre (SIRC)** — research focused on uplifting communities and developing solutions to social and environmental problems.
- **Aerospace innovation** — applying emerging technologies (including next-generation landing-gear development) to aircraft maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO), tied to the Downsview Campus aerospace hub and industry partners such as Bombardier and Safran.
- **Advanced manufacturing** — leveraging new technologies to improve manufacturing productivity, efficiency, and commercialization potential for partners.
- **Renewable energy and microgrid technology** — next-generation energy storage, optimization, and microgrid work, with expertise spanning solar and wind, tied to the Centennial Energy Institute.
- **Centre of Innovation and Entrepreneurship (COIE)** — see 8.4 below.

Internally, ARIES also runs the **Applied Research and Innovation Fund (ARIF)**, which funds faculty-led applied-research projects, letting the principal investigator (rather than an external industry partner) drive project direction — the college notes this flexibility lets ARIF-funded outcomes flow directly back into capstone-course curriculum, benefiting future student cohorts. All Centennial Innovates activity is explicitly mapped against the **United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** framework.

### 8.4 Centre of Innovation and Entrepreneurship (COIE)

COIE is the entrepreneurship-focused hub within Centennial Innovates, aimed at students, alumni, staff, and the broader community.

- **Location:** Progress Campus, **Office B-206**, 941 Progress Avenue
- **Email:** entrepreneurship@centennialcollege.ca
- **Website:** accel.centennialcollege.ca

COIE delivers **60+ free public workshops per year** on building sustainable, lifelong entrepreneurial skills, and offers structured, multi-track programming that can also satisfy a student's **work-integrated learning requirement** (Field Placement, Internship, or Co-op Placement) in an applicable program. Program tracks include:

- **Design Sprint / Weekend Challenge** — short, intensive innovation-challenge events (e.g., the "Global Goals Jam" series).
- **Rapid Start** — entrepreneurship-basics workshop series for aspiring solo entrepreneurs/freelancers.
- **Incubation Program** — a structured, roughly 10–12-week program pairing participants with one-on-one mentoring to move an idea toward actual operations.
- **Business Innovation and Transformation Program** — for existing small/medium enterprises seeking to build capacity in business-model innovation, strategy, and digital transformation.
- **Market Readiness Program** — helps participants build a go-to-market strategy for commercializing a technology.

COIE also operates a **VR Lab**, used in some programming for experiential activities such as practising public speaking/presentation skills in a simulated environment. COIE partners with Coursera (through "Coursera for Careers") to give Centennial *alumni* free access (time-limited promotional windows apply — confirm current offer) to a wide range of self-paced courses and industry-recognized professional certificates from providers such as Google, IBM, Meta, and Microsoft.

### 9.1 Identity and Conference Affiliation

Centennial's varsity athletics program competes as the **Centennial Colts** (colours: green; mascot: **Bolt the Colt**). The Colts compete in the **Ontario Colleges Athletic Association (OCAA)**, specifically within the OCAA's **East Region**, and Colts teams are also affiliated at the national level with the **Canadian Collegiate Athletic Association (CCAA)**. (Note: some older or third-party summaries list only "CCAA" as the affiliation; the OCAA is the direct conference Centennial competes in week-to-week, with the CCAA serving as the national umbrella body for Canadian college athletics — CampusAI should reference both accurately rather than only one.)

### 9.2 Varsity Sports

As of the confirmed 2026–27 varsity schedule published by Colts Athletics, Centennial fields the following intercollegiate varsity teams:

- **Men's Basketball** and **Women's Basketball**
- **Men's Soccer** and **Women's Soccer** (outdoor, fall season) — Centennial also fields **Men's Indoor Soccer** and **Women's Indoor Soccer**, competing in tournaments leading into OCAA Regional and Provincial Indoor Championships (typically culminating around March).

Historical program notes: Centennial's Men's Basketball program has a long competitive history dating to the earliest days of the OCAA, including national and OCAA medal finishes across multiple decades (e.g., OCAA Gold in 1982–83 and 1983–84, Bronze in 1980–81, Silver in 1987–88 and 2004–05). ### 9.5 Student Association and Wellness Overlap (CCSAI)

While not part of Colts Athletics itself, the **Centennial College Student Association Inc. (CCSAI)** is worth noting alongside campus recreation and wellness, since it operates the **Progress Student Centre** and co-manages elements of the **Athletic and Wellness Centre**, both of which were constructed using student-levy funding. CCSAI was the **first student association at any Ontario college**, established in 1967 and formally incorporated in the 1980s. It represents all full-time and part-time fee-paying Centennial students and offers services including a food bank and grab-and-go breakfast program, a domestic student health/dental plan, clubs and campus events, job/volunteer postings, a confidential student **Advocacy service** (helping students navigate appeals, policy questions, and disputes with faculty, including accompanying a student to a meeting if requested), used textbooks/classifieds, and legal-referral services.
